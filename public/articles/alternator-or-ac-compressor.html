Alternator or A/C compressor? What to inspect

Both parts may be metal, belt-driven accessories mounted low in an engine bay. The pulley alone is not enough. Look for the type of connection, housing design, printed label, and location before choosing a name.

Scan the visible part with Deep SpecReview the probable result and evidence, then save a correction when the part is checked.

Clues that support an alternator

  • A heavy electrical output terminal, usually protected by a cap, plus a smaller control connector.
  • A vented aluminum housing where copper windings may be visible through openings.
  • A pulley driven by the accessory belt, without two large refrigerant pipe connections entering the case.
  • A label that may show electrical ratings, manufacturer information, or an alternator part number.

Clues that support an A/C compressor

  • Two refrigerant line connections or a manifold block attached to the compressor body.
  • A cylindrical or compact pump-like case with mounting ears and a belt pulley.
  • On some designs, a visible clutch plate at the pulley face; other variable compressors may not use an obvious external clutch.
  • A refrigerant or compressor label. Do not loosen a line to inspect it.

What a photo still cannot confirm

Appearance does not prove exact fitment, internal function, refrigerant charge, electrical output, or whether the part is safe to reuse. Record the label, vehicle details, connector side, and mounting location. Verify replacement decisions with the VIN, part number, service information, and appropriate testing.

Keep hands, clothing, and tools away from a moving belt. Do not open an A/C system or disconnect a battery circuit without the correct service procedure.
