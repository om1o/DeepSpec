How to photograph a car part for identification

A useful identification photo does more than make the object large. It preserves the clues that separate similar parts: where the item is installed, what connects to it, how it is mounted, and what text is printed or stamped on it.

Take these three photos

  1. Overall view: show the complete part and enough of the engine bay, wheel area, or underside to understand its location. Do not crop away every neighboring component.
  2. Connector side: show electrical plugs, hose fittings, mounting ears, ports, pulleys, or belt routing. Move your hand out of the way after supporting the part safely.
  3. Readable label: move close enough to capture the part number, manufacturer name, casting mark, QR code, or warning label. Tap to focus and check the text before leaving.

Light and focus matter

Wipe the camera lens. Use steady, indirect light instead of a flashlight reflection on metal. Hold the phone still, keep the intended part near the center, and retake the image when the label smears or the object occupies only a small corner.

Add context a photo cannot prove

Record the vehicle year, make, model, engine, and the area where the part came from. A photo may support a probable part family, but exact fitment often requires a VIN, readable number, service information, or physical measurement.

Do not reach near a running engine, hot exhaust, moving belt, pressurized line, raised vehicle, or damaged high-voltage component just to improve a photo.
